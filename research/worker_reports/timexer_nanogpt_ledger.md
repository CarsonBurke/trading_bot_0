## Critical port discrepancies, ordered by confidence rather than promised NLL benefit

1. **Attention output is initialized differently, and our explanation is factually wrong.** `M:486` invokes `zeroed_projection`; `M:337-346` sets zero. `G:1293` uniformly initializes **all real V and O matrices**; only padding is zero at1294. `G:1161` uses O directly from that bank. `CastedLinearT` is used at `G:1221` for LM head. This is not subtle uncertainty: our `M:323-327` conflates different modules. It can alter initial gradient propagation (zero O delays QKV learning); it does not demonstrate that turning O on will improve held-out finance loss.
2. **MLP down LR is four times lower relative to base than current speedrun.** Both upstream matrices are stored tall; its shape multiplier2 and down multiplier2 produce4. Our wide down matrix gets1. Changing storage is mathematically neutral to GEMM but not to a shape-derived optimizer. Our up multiplier2 matches. Trunk down contains8,388,608 weights. A matched low-cost LR-only audit can test this without increasing model FLOPs; *do not presume that faster fitting is desirable*.
3. **Packed QKV creates different optimizer competition.** Our [1536,512] matrix is jointly orthogonalized and aspect-scaled sqrt3. Upstream separately orthogonalizes Q/K head pairs [128,512] for our dimensions and V [512,512], with aspect1. Joint orthogonalization changes singular directions, not just LR. Numerical correction requires separate controls; do not change grouping and LR simultaneously and call it an ablation.
4. **Residual `post` lambda folding is not upstream SA-lambda folding.** Current upstream has QKV scalar .5 and O scalar1 (`G:1341-1344,1103,1161`) **in addition to** post/residual lambdas. We do not have that QKV scalar. Folding our post lambda into weights moves a float multiplication across bf16 GEMM accumulation. `S/2026-02-16_FlattenForward:45-50` reports that this historical rearrangement hurt its loss. Exact-real algebra is not bf16 training equivalence. That record does not prove our observed overfit comes from folding.
5. **Historical/full-U/static x0/first-V versus current sparse/dynamic mechanisms.** We have a coherent old-recipe hybrid, not current GPT. The full U stores four gates whereas current sparse skip also has token-dependent modulation. The static first-V pathway costs real traffic and is not equivalent to learned token-value retrieval. Do not label absence of current dynamic gates a bug.
6. **Adam beta/cadence treatment differs.** Current scalars include .99 second moment and head/embed .5 first moment, ours global .9/.95. Ours every-step Adam means approximately twice as many head/scalar update and decay opportunities per same optimizer-step count as current LM's every-other-step scheme; gradient distributions differ, so not an exact factor-of-two effective LR. Accumulating Adam is forbidden here.
7. **Head and normalization numerical choices differ deliberately.** Our zero Gaussian head plus1/32 scale and fp32 softcapped log sigma is not the nonzero categorical sigmoid-capped head. Our fixed1e-6 RMS epsilon and NorMuon variance denominator differ. No evidence licenses treating these all as identical μP.
8. **Trunk weight decay exists.** At base .023, square-matrix quadratic decay coefficient before cautious mask is1.2×.023²=.0006348 per step; up FFN with aspect2 is .0012696. At .008, head/patch/covariate coefficient is .005×150×.008²=.000048. At LR floor .15, these shrink to2.25% of initial coefficient. A cautious mask only decays a subset; these coefficients are not global weight norm changes. Adam fallback is WD0 (`C:471`), but the measured recipe is Polar Express. Therefore “absence of trunk WD caused overfit” is false for this recipe.

## Accelerator inversion: what the new measurements do and do not establish

### Supplied measurements, accepted without re-running

Every configuration overfits inside the first epoch. Four completed arms peak at2k then worsen:
- scalar-LR multiplier1: heldout NLL1.9965→2.0863.
- x0 disabled:1.9973→2.0669.
- both:1.9972→2.0689.
- global LR.004: worst at5k,2.2207.

On x0-disabled,2k→5k market-neutral MSE ratios are:
- h1: .9636→.9602 (improves).
- h8: .9458→.9256 (improves).
- h64: .9886→1.0688 (rots).
- h192:1.0257→1.0854 (rots).

At5k coverage .678/.923 versus nominal .683/.950 is consistent with the supplied conclusion that this is not sigma collapse. The supplied cross-term moves h1−.070→−.011,h8−.108→−.004, but h64−.007→−.073. Conditional-mean fitting is becoming harmful along the horizon axis. Earlier supplied short-horizon IC and transaction-cost outcomes remain unchanged; this audit does not re-estimate them.

**DEMONSTRATED from code:** all horizon outputs share one backbone and head hidden activation (`M:1578-1593`); channel/horizon contributions are accumulated into one count-normalized Gaussian objective (`M:1700-1720`). It includes the appropriate1/h precision prior, but that is variance normalization, **not predictive-SNR weighting**. All horizons have learnable mean coordinates; the long close coordinate is scaled bysqrt(h) (`M:1688-1698`). Thus, under the sigma-sqrt(h) prior, larger-h normalization does not automatically make a low-SNR mean irrelevant to shared-trunk training.

**HYPOTHESIS, not yet causal proof:** memorized long-horizon training-period means exert harmful gradients on shared features. The horizon-specific deterioration strongly supports pursuing this diagnosis, but observing shared parameters and deterioration does not itself establish that long-horizon gradients caused short-horizon representation harm—short horizons are still improving. A weighting/capacity-separation intervention is required for that causal claim.

### Which techniques preferentially accelerate weak target directions?

1. **NorMuon/polar spectral equalization and row/u-w floors are the strongest mechanistic candidates.** Polar iteration deliberately replaces singular-value magnitudes with a much flatter spectrum; NorMuon then normalizes per-neuron variance while preserving global update norm. This can make weak gradient directions relatively larger than ordinary magnitude-following updates. If low-SNR long-horizon correlations populate weak directions, fitting them can accelerate. **But spectral direction ≠ horizon:** this correspondence is a hypothesis, not demonstrated by the source. RowUpdateFloor is even more explicit: `T:123-126` says small update/weight row ratios are amplified to a floor. It is NOT currently in ours. Cheapest falsifier for the selectivity hypothesis: capture horizon-group gradient/update alignment/norm statistics at matched checkpoints, then one horizon-weighting run; optimizer family replacement alone confounds many variables.
2. **Late batch-size growth can favor weak/noisy patterns.** Its own justification says early training fits common patterns while late training fits rarer ones, whose gradients remain noisy (`S/2025-11-29_BatchSizeSchedule:42-48`). Lower gradient noise can reveal useful rare signal OR coherently fit train-period idiosyncrasies. Ours does not currently ramp batches, so it cannot explain the present failure. Cheapest falsifier if proposed: N09 with equal examples and no LR bump.
3. **Growing attention context, bigram/token-value lookup capacity and MUDD introduce additional paths to rare/local associations.** Long-context keys can match training-period structure; lookups memorize frequent/rare identities efficiently. This is a mechanism for fitting inputs, not proof of long-horizon selectivity. Most current lookup/dynamic machinery is absent/inapplicable, so cannot explain current failure. A ticker embedding must not be smuggled in as a “port.”

### Which adopted techniques are uniform fitting accelerators, not horizon selectors?

- **Residual/post/x0 lambdas, UNet skips, first-value residual** shorten optimization paths, preserve/reuse early information, and permit large residual-stream changes with few scalars. The UNet record explicitly paired skips with **doubling LR** (`S/2024-11-10_UNetDoubleLr:7-9`). They have no horizon index or loss-SNR knowledge. Their possible benefit inversion is that easier optimization reaches period-specific mean fitting sooner, **not** that they inherently target h64. The completed x0/scalar ablations directly refute blaming one of these controls as the sole culprit.
- **High base/effective per-family LR and long plateau** continue substantial fitting updates after simple shared patterns are learned. The schedule is tuned against a1270-step LM endpoint; our endpoint9590 keeps full LR until3836 despite best heldout NLL at2000. This is a genuine mismatch. But halving global LR was worse, so “just lower LR” is empirically refuted; split schedule may only help if different parts are at different learning stages.
- **Zero/nonzero branch init and readout scaling, QK/RMS normalization, ReLU²** change conditioning, gradient propagation and stable effective learning rate. None provides direct predictive-SNR allocation. Correcting a port discrepancy may improve optimization fidelity while worsening generalization.
- **Cautious quadratic WD** is not absent and is not purely an accelerator. It constrains norms in a coordinate-dependent way, and its historical README warns validation can lag most of training and only catch up late (`S/2025-11-10_CautiousWD:46-59`). Our delayed cooldown therefore deserves scrutiny, but shrinking WD with LR also weakens late regularization. Do not infer that stronger WD is automatically beneficial under RMS normalization and shape-dependent effective steps.
- **Kernel fusion, CUDA graphs, copy elimination, bf16/FP8 throughput, distributed communication** are computational accelerators. At fixed steps/examples and equivalent numerics their statistical benefit does not invert. They only make overfitting arrive sooner in wall time if the saved time is automatically spent taking more steps. Preserve that distinction.

### The single-epoch premise needs correction

The ordinary short speedrun consumes a forward stream of shards (`G:1810-1879`) rather than repeated epochs, but **no repeated examples does not make overfitting impossible**. Repeated token types/patterns, finite-sample noise, benchmark hyperparameter selection and distribution shift still exist. The repo also explicitly contains a5×10B versus50B comparison (`S/2024-11-04_50Bruns:3-24`), including a warning that WSD/zero-WD tuned to speedrun duration may be suboptimal for longer training. No need to dispute the financial observations: time-period shift and the observed horizon split are sufficient evidence that our statistical objective differs from the LM speedrun objective.

## Does the speedrun solve different-SNR targets or separate their capacity?

**No, not in the inspected code.** There is no demonstrated predictive-SNR-aware weighting and no separate trunks/heads for target groups of different SNR.

The closest *surface resemblance* is MTP annealing: one vocabulary distribution is encouraged to predict the next few tokens, then auxiliary farther-token weights are annealed away. Prefix prediction adds lexical vocabulary structure and is likewise annealed away. They share the same representation and readout; they do not partition capacity by target difficulty. Our192 distinct horizon mean/scale outputs are a substantially different objective. Calling upstream MTP a ready-made solution for our long-horizon overfit would stretch the analogy.

A financial horizon-weighting experiment (N22) is consequently an explicitly **new hypothesis** informed by the supplied measurements, not an ADOPTED or demonstrated speedrun trick. Keep evaluation original equal-horizon NLL to prevent a tautological win. If capacity separation is desired later, separating output columns alone changes nothing—they already have separate weights. One must separate hidden capacity or shared-trunk gradient contributions, which requires an explicit costed architecture design outside this ledger's exact-port remit.

## Priorities

1. **Difficulty-aware horizon pressure (N22, new adaptation):** highest hypothesized heldout-NLL gain per added compute because the measured failure is horizon-specific and cost is essentiallyzero. Not a claim that speedrun already implements it.
2. **Tail-EMA readout (N24):** low-cost, reversible evaluation of trajectory drift at~.4–1ms/step; may damp amplitude overfit while retaining improving short-horizon features. It cannot recover truly absent long-horizon signal and can also blur useful learning.
3. **Independent matrix/head schedule budget (N04):**~zero compute, directly audits3836-versus2000 cooldown mismatch. Do not repeat the refuted global-LR-halving or scalar-LR arms.

For speed rather than NLL, prioritize **exact Gaussian head/loss fusion (N18)** over LM-specific sparse embedding/collective tricks: our explicit head/loss traffic estimate alone is20.275GB/step, and removing materializations changes no capacity. The cost estimates justify investigating it but are not measured speedups.

**Bottom line:** current code demonstrably differs from the modern speedrun in ways worth correcting in its documentation and isolating experimentally. Those differences do not overturn the completed negative arms. The strongest task-specific evidence is a shared equal-horizon objective learning short-horizon signal while fitting harmful long-horizon means. A faithful modernization that merely accelerates the same objective can make this worse, not better.
