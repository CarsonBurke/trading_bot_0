# NextLat and richer OHLC targets: decision report

## Verdict and evidence conventions

**HYPOTHESIS — REJECT NextLat as the production answer to the observed failure.** It is applicable as a training-only representation regularizer, not as a richer return distribution or a replacement for our direct forecast head. Its best demonstrated wins are state tracking and planning in worlds with stable transition rules. Its large language-model test actually worsens held-out perplexity. The cheapest defensible challenge to this rejection is one 5,000-step, one-step Smooth-L1-only auxiliary run, described below. No autoregressive inference is needed.

## 1. What NextLat actually predicts

**DEMONSTRATED — Mechanism:** Let h_t be the last normalized transformer state and e(x_{t+1}) the embedding of the *observed next token*. NextLat predicts h-hat_{t+1}=h_t+MLP(LN(concat[e(x_{t+1}),h_t])). Its regression target is stop-gradient h_{t+1} from the same ordinary causal transformer forward. This is learning how to update a state **after seeing the next observation**, not forecasting the unpredictable next observation without seeing it. The teacher is the current model, not a separate EMA encoder.

**DEMONSTRATED — Quoted loss, paper §3.2 Eqs. 3–4, with notation simplified but terms unchanged:**

> L_next-h = E_t[(1/d) sum_{i=1}^d SmoothL1Loss(sg[h_{t+i}], h-hat_{t+i})]
>
> L_KL = E_t[(1/d) sum_{i=1}^d KL(p_theta^sg(.|sg[h_{t+i}]) || p_theta^sg(.|h-hat_{t+i}))]
>
> L = L_next-token + lambda_next-h L_next-h + lambda_KL L_KL.

**DEMONSTRATED — Code detail:** `models/model_nextlat.py:284–330` calls `F.smooth_l1_loss`, despite variables/comments named MSE. With PyTorch's default beta=1, the scalar penalty is 0.5 r² for |r|<1 and |r|−0.5 otherwise. Target states and teacher logits are detached; auxiliary output-head weights are detached, but derivatives through the student's state remain. `:332–410` recursively applies the same dynamics MLP using teacher-forced observed tokens for d steps. `:484–496` averages across d and adds the normal token CE; an optional auxiliary CE defaults to zero. CE is nevertheless computed for logging, and README explicitly says to remove that computation to reproduce its speed measurements.

**DEMONSTRATED — Architecture:** the implementation uses an ordinary decoder-only causal transformer with RoPE and final LayerNorm, plus a three-linear-layer residual dynamics MLP, GELU between layers and LayerNorm on the concatenated input. For model width D its input is 2D; hidden width is `128*round(proj_factor*2D/128)`; output is D. The MLP predicts a deterministic latent vector, not Gaussian parameters, quantiles, a flow velocity or a quantized token. Ordinary inference ignores this branch. Optional speculative decoding recursively uses it, but that is a separate inference feature, not a prerequisite.

**HYPOTHESIS — Relevance relative to our objective:** nothing about NextLat requires replacing our Gaussian NLL or our 192-horizon direct block. It adds recurrent-state consistency alongside them. Our existing dense multi-horizon supervision already addresses much of the paper's complaint about exclusively myopic next-token supervision; it does not already impose recurrent-state consistency.

## 3. Why the transfer is uncertain under our actual failure

**DEMONSTRATED — Supplied measurements, not remeasured:** the latest nox0 arm improves h=1 MSE ratio from 0.9636 at 2k to 0.9602 at 5k, and h=8 from 0.9458 to 0.9256, while h=64 worsens 0.9886→1.0688 and h=192 1.0257→1.0854. The mean mis-scaling cross term improves at h=1 (−0.070→−0.011) and h=8 (−0.108→−0.004), but worsens at h=64 (−0.007→−0.073). Coverage at 5k is 0.678/0.923 versus nominal 0.683/0.950. Thus rising aggregate NLL is not evidence that short-horizon means are failing or that sigma collapsed. Long-horizon mean amplitude is the target failure; 95% coverage still has a modest deficit, not a demonstrated dominant problem.

**HYPOTHESIS — Best argument for NextLat:** a compact causal state update might discourage attention lookups that memorize period-specific configurations. Because e(next patch) is observed during auxiliary training, it need not predict unpredictable noise to learn a stable volatility/liquidity update. This is a plausible regularization mechanism even in a low-SNR process.

**HYPOTHESIS — Stronger counterargument:** a compact state can just as easily encode ticker, session and training-period regime, or focus on volatility that is already learned. Compressing hidden states is not the same as selectively shrinking the long-horizon conditional mean. The same self-teacher that overpredicts long-horizon returns can teach those errors through output KL. Adding stronger joint likelihoods or more expressive distributions likewise cannot manufacture stable return information.

**DEMONSTRATED — The theorem is conditional:** Theorem 3.2 requires exact next-token conditional-distribution consistency and exact transition consistency with respect to an underlying distribution. It establishes sufficiency under those conditions, not convergence of stochastic optimization, finite-sample regularization, minimal dimension, or generalization to a different market period. A restricted Gaussian output does not identify an arbitrary full future-observation distribution.

**HYPOTHESIS — Target normalization matters:** keep the persistence anchor, causal sigma and beta adjustment. They already remove large nuisance level/scale/market effects. A state-update loss is not a reason to restore raw price levels or apply whole-window ReVIN. Adjacent-origin targets have different anchors and causal normalizers; latent matching compares states, not numerically shifted forecast blocks. A KL comparison, if used, must decode student and teacher with the **same next-origin** normalization and known-covariate inputs. Never compare h_t's horizon k directly with h_{t+1}'s horizon k as if they were the same target.

**HYPOTHESIS — SNR interpretation:** IC≈0.03 does not prohibit useful state compression, but it makes volatility/calendar predictability much easier for a latent objective to exploit than small tradable mean differences. Do not square this cross-sectional IC and present it as the pooled explained variance; they are different measurements. The supplied h=1 spread/cost result is underwater near 1.2 bps/side, whereas h=8's stronger point skill and longer return scale motivate evaluating h=8 net utility first. A higher MSE skill at h=8 is not itself proof of larger net P&L; holdings, turnover, serial dependence and actual executable prices must be measured.

**DEMONSTRATED — Sundial contrast:** our existing analysis and Sundial Table 3 give average point MSE 0.290 for TimeFlow versus 0.296 for MSE, with MSE winning two of six datasets; no heteroscedastic-Gaussian comparison. NextLat is not that mechanism. Near-nominal coverage and long-mean over-amplitude are reasons not to prioritize a flow sampler. I do not inherit the old lens's 'multi-epoch training is pragmatic' inference: the supplied newer period-overfit measurements supersede that inference.

## 4. Concrete mapping and honest cost accounting

**HYPOTHESIS — Accounting model for all proposals:** B=256, T=375, N=96,000 origins, D=512, H=192, C=4, K=HC=768. Baseline supplied: 16.98 TFLOP/step, 143.20 GB/step, 168.59 ms/step, approximately 92 achieved TFLOPS. Decimal GB/TFLOP are used. An added dense D→q trainable projection costs 512q weights and approximately 6NDq = 0.000294912q TFLOP/step (forward, input gradient, weight gradient). One added output tensor is Nq×2–4 bytes. For a working traffic budget assume eight aggregate elementwise reads/writes per added scalar across forward/backward: Δtraffic≈0.001536q–0.003072q GB; this is an explicit materialization model, not a DRAM measurement. Add small parameter/state traffic and separate nonlinear/linalg work. Effective supplied bandwidth is 143.20/168.59=0.849 GB/ms. Compute-only Δtime≈10.87 ms/added TFLOP at 92 TFLOPS; traffic-only Δtime≈1.18 ms/added GB. Neither predicts measured latency exactly; kernel launch, shape efficiency, fusion and overlap matter. Listed time ranges are engineering acceptance budgets, not benchmark results.

**HYPOTHESIS — Minimal useful NextLat variant:** leave all eight blocks and the 512→1536 Gaussian head unchanged. Attach a training-only residual MLP to final normalized states: concat(h_i, patch_embedding_{i+1})→1024→256→256→512, with GELUs, a gainless input normalization consistent with our model, and residual h_i. Predict 512 latent coordinates; target `detach(h_{i+1})`. Reuse the already causal teacher forward; exclude row ends/invalid transitions. Set d=1, lambda_next-h=1 on the coordinate-mean Smooth L1 and lambda_KL=0. Gradients flow into current state and next patch embedding, never through the detached target; the supervised forecast at i is still computed without next-patch information. It is auxiliary supervision, not a future-input leak into inference. No EMA model, extra transformer pass, sampling, recurrent inference, accumulation or chunking.

**HYPOTHESIS — Variant cost:** 1024×256+256²+256×512 = **458,752 added weights** (0.459M), plus zero gainless-normalization parameters; about **0.264 TFLOP/step**, making **17.244 TFLOP/step (+1.55%)**. With approximately 3–6 full forward/backward passes over the input/intermediate/latent buffers, reserve **+2–5 GB/step**, total **145.2–148.2 GB (+1.4–3.5%)**. Added parameter/gradient/AdamW-state storage is roughly 6–8 MB depending on precision. Compute/traffic projections support an initial **+4–10 ms/step** acceptance budget, total **173–179 ms**, not 'free'. Use AdamW on these auxiliary weights for the cost-controlled experiment; adding them to a matrix-orthogonalization group requires separately pricing its updates. Deploying the model drops the auxiliary MLP, so inference parameter/FLOP/traffic deltas are zero.

## 5. The single cheapest falsifying training experiment

**HYPOTHESIS — Run:** train the 256-wide, d=1, Smooth-L1-only auxiliary variant from the **same initialization and data order as the existing nox0 control**, with the control optimizer/schedule/head and unaltered batch 256, to **5,000 steps maximum**. One seed, one arm; do not resume at 2k because the proposed mechanism is representation formation. Use the existing control trajectory if truly matched; otherwise a matched control is necessary before causal attribution. This tests a cheap NextLat variant, not every NextLat hyperparameter. At 173–179 ms/step, 5k training steps budget approximately 14.4–14.9 minutes plus evaluation; no run was performed here.

**HYPOTHESIS — Named report series at matched 2k/3k/4k/5k checkpoints:** `held_out_sample_nll`, `market_neutral_mse_ratio_h1`, `market_neutral_mse_ratio_h64`, `within_1sigma_coverage`, and `train_minus_held_out_nll` (training NLL minus held-out NLL; a growing negative magnitude is a worsening generalization gap). Also report `market_neutral_mse_ratio_h8`, `mean_misscaling_cross_term_h64`, `cross_sectional_ic_h8`, and `net_spread_bps_h8_cost_1bps_per_side`/`...2bps_per_side`. The auxiliary loss is a diagnostic only and must never be added to the comparable evaluation NLL. Measure day-block uncertainty on the same held-out observations, not IID origin standard errors over overlapping 192-horizon labels.

**HYPOTHESIS — Precommitted decision:** overturn rejection only if the new arm materially arrests the 2k→5k h64 degradation, brings 5k h64 below persistence (ratio<1.00 rather than control 1.0688), keeps h1/h8 no worse within day-block uncertainty, improves common held-out NLL and reduces the magnitude of the negative train-minus-held-out gap without worsening |coverage−0.683|. Require improved net h8 utility under the same frozen trading rule to claim *trading* usefulness. ## 6. Richer targets ranked against long-horizon mean over-amplitude

**HYPOTHESIS — Ranking principle:** expected trading value per added compute, conditional on the supplied failure. 'Can represent joint uncertainty' and 'fixes long-horizon mean overfit' are separate columns mentally. The first two proposals change the economically relevant optimization or constrain the means; most remaining proposals chiefly improve uncertainty. Each experiment below is an alternative single matched run to ≤5k, not a request to launch a sweep.

### 1. Cost-aware cross-sectional portfolio utility, h=8 first

**DEMONSTRATED — External precedent:** [Zhang, Zohren & Roberts, Deep Learning for Portfolio Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13665) trains portfolio weights directly against Sharpe rather than first optimizing expected-return prediction. Its setting is ETF portfolios and is not evidence of intraday 4,873-stock profitability.

**HYPOTHESIS — Design/value:** use the existing h8 close mean to form differentiable dollar-neutral weights and optimize negative realized net utility, e.g. L_trade=−sum_i w_i r_i + c sum_i smooth_abs(w_i−w_i_prev) + a fixed risk penalty. Weights need bounded gross exposure and actual executable return units; costs are per-side turnover, not a penalty against sigma-scaled target magnitude. Add this to the retained observation likelihood so the requested sample NLL remains meaningful. This can redirect shared features toward the horizon with demonstrated skill and away from useless activity; it does not mathematically guarantee fixing long-horizon means. Previous weights require consecutive **calendar** portfolios, not arbitrary shuffled examples. Mean/variance decisions cannot be evaluated with a turnover penalty using unrelated rows.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cost:** no new model weights, **<0.001 TFLOP/step** for O(N) score/weight/cost arithmetic and fixed-rank neutrality projection; **+0.01–0.1 GB/step**, **+0.1–1 ms** if equal-sized timestamp-aligned ticker subsets and previous-period data already fit in the existing B×T pass. Those are strict conditions, not a free data-layout assumption. Full-universe rows at each of 375 origins require at least ceil(4873/256)=20× the row compute, roughly **339.6 TFLOP, 2864 GB and 3372 ms per equivalent full-universe pass** at unchanged efficiency; reject that as the first training experiment. A timestamp-aligned 256-ticker subset objective is an explicit estimator, not the full-universe objective. An h8 turnover term needing separately forwarded previous windows adds up to a full extra trunk pass and is also rejected unless consecutive origins can be gathered from existing rows.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cheapest falsifier:** before retraining, evaluate the existing 2k and 5k h8 forecasts with one fixed exposure/turnover rule on chronological full-universe inference. If even a cost-aware rule yields no net advantage, a fancier loss has a weaker case. Then, only if the data layout allows the stated cheap exact-calendar subset construction, one matched ≤5k auxiliary-utility run; falsify with no improvement in out-of-period net bps at 1/2 bps per side or with materially degraded h1/h8 point skill. Smooth absolute-value costs must be scored with actual absolute turnover at evaluation.

### 2. Joint horizon representation with a low-rank MEAN restriction—not just covariance

**HYPOTHESIS — Design/value:** preserve independent h1…h8 means; represent h9…h192 means using eight fixed smooth/decaying horizon basis functions per channel. This changes the long-horizon mean's representational freedom while preserving the short band that continues to learn. The fixed basis should be specified before seeing validation results; zero coefficients still mean persistence. This is explicitly a structured-mean variant of the requested joint-path proposal, not a claim that low-rank covariance alone regularizes means. It can address the demonstrated failure but may underfit genuinely horizon-specific alpha.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cost:** replace 768 mean outputs with 4×8 direct short means +4×8 basis coefficients=64 outputs, keeping 768 scales. Δq=−704; **−360,448 weights**, **−0.2076 TFLOP/step** before approximately 0.0034 TFLOP for forward/backward basis expansion. Output likelihood still consumes all 768 means, so do **not** claim all dense-target traffic disappears. Reserve **−0.5 to +0.5 GB/step**, and **−3 to +1 ms** pending fusion; total about **16.776 TFLOP, 142.7–143.7 GB, 166–170 ms**. Static matrix expansion is capturable and emits the whole block without rollout.

**HYPOTHESIS — Falsifier:** one ≤5k matched run; reject if h64 mean over-amplitude and ratio do not improve versus control, if h1/h8 lose useful skill, or if h8 net returns deteriorate. This proposal is not established by NextLat; it is a direct response to the new horizon-dependent measurement.

### 3. Gaussian CRPS on the same head

**HYPOTHESIS — Design/value:** keep Gaussian parameters but replace training NLL with its analytic CRPS. For z=(y−mu)/s, CRPS=s[z(2Phi(z)−1)+2phi(z)−1/sqrt(pi)]. Its mean gradient is bounded (2Phi(z)−1), unlike residual divided by variance under NLL; it changes error weighting without increasing density expressiveness. This might reduce destabilizing long-horizon gradients, but does not selectively identify training-period memorization. It is less aligned to MSE than MSE itself, so point improvement is uncertain.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cost/falsifier:** **0 added weights**, approximately **<0.005 TFLOP/step** excluding special-function latency, **0–2 GB/step** incremental traffic depending on fusion, acceptance budget **0–4 ms/step**. Evaluate common Gaussian sample NLL plus all named series. One ≤5k matched run falsifies usefulness if common NLL/coverage improve but h64 point over-amplitude or h8 net utility do not. The smallest preliminary test is computing CRPS and its per-horizon gradient magnitudes on already saved predictions; it is diagnostic, not training evidence.

### 4. Joint four-channel OHLC Gaussian via Cholesky

**DEMONSTRATED — Distribution identity:** a four-dimensional Gaussian needs four means and ten lower-triangular scale entries; Σ=LLᵀ yields correlation that four independent marginals cannot express. [PyTorch multivariate-normal documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/distributions.html#multivariatenormal) recommends a supplied `scale_tril` to avoid repeatedly decomposing a covariance matrix.

**HYPOTHESIS — Design/value:** retain four means, emit six new off-diagonal entries per horizon, positive diagonal scales; use joint Gaussian NLL, normalized per coordinate. This represents close/open/range error relationships and changes mean gradients through Σ⁻¹. It does not enforce valid candles and does not necessarily correct long-horizon over-amplitude; likelihood may improve solely by fitting correlation. These within-ticker channel correlations are not cross-ticker portfolio-risk correlations.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cost:** q=6×192=1152 extra outputs: **+589,824 weights**, **+0.3397 TFLOP** projection work plus **<0.02 TFLOP** fixed 4×4 solves/logdet arithmetic. Added raw output storage **0.221–0.442 GB**; budget **+2–5 GB/step** total incremental traffic, **+5–10 ms** including small-matrix inefficiency. Approximate total **17.34 TFLOP, 145.2–148.2 GB, 174–179 ms**. Explicit fixed-size triangular arithmetic avoids expensive millions-of-tiny-library-call dispatch; static batched implementation is capturable in principle.

**HYPOTHESIS — Falsifier:** cheapest diagnostic is fit a fixed four-channel residual correlation matrix on training predictions and assess held-out joint NLL, not validation-fitted correlation. Then one ≤5k learned-Cholesky run only if dependence transfers. Reject an alpha claim if h64 means and h8 net utility do not improve even when joint NLL does. Compare **marginal** NLL as the common report series; a joint likelihood is not numerically the old independent likelihood.

### 5. Joint horizon/path covariance, low-rank factors

**DEMONSTRATED — Distribution identity:** Σ=diag(v)+UUᵀ represents shared path factors. [PyTorch LowRankMultivariateNormal](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/distributions.html#lowrankmultivariatenormal) uses Woodbury/determinant identities to reduce the expensive inverse/determinant to the factor-rank capacitance matrix.

**HYPOTHESIS — Design/value:** model the complete 768-vector with rank r=4, keeping current means/scales and adding U∈R^(768×4). This expresses coherent cross-horizon and cross-channel uncertainty, useful for horizon differences, risk, stops and drawdown calculations. Covariance-only factors leave all 768 means unconstrained. They can absorb a systematic error as correlated uncertainty rather than fix over-amplitude, so rank below the structured-mean variant. A four-factor approximation may not capture cumulative-return path covariance well; evaluate against the simple random-walk covariance, not just diagonal noise.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cost:** q=3072: **+1,572,864 weights**, **+0.9060 TFLOP** projection plus order NKr² linear-algebra work (reserve **<0.02 TFLOP** at r=4); raw factor output **0.590–1.180 GB**; traffic budget **+5–12 GB/step**, latency budget **+12–25 ms**. Total approximately **17.91 TFLOP, 148.2–155.2 GB, 181–194 ms**. An unrestricted 768×768 Cholesky instead adds 294,528 outputs above diagonal scales, approximately **150.8M weights, 86.86 TFLOP** projection alone, and **56.5–113.1 GB** for one raw output tensor: decisively reject.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cheapest falsifier:** fit a **global** rank-four residual factor matrix on the training period and evaluate frozen held-out path scores and fixed path-sensitive decisions first. This has 3072 fitted factor entries and no dynamic head; low-rank scoring is order NKr², approximately <0.02 TFLOP and <1 GB extra traffic, roughly 0–3 ms per scoring pass. This tests transferable dependence, not the need for a learned conditional factor head. Reject learned factors if only joint density improves while h64 MSE and actual path-dependent utility remain unchanged. No OHLC bar model identifies the intrabar ordering of high and low; stop/take-profit backtests must retain that ambiguity.

### 6. Bar-native coordinates respecting OHLC order

**HYPOTHESIS — Design/value:** four degrees of freedom are required for a complete candle—not just a three-number tuple. One continuous-interior construction uses close log-price c, log range a with R=exp(a), and logits u/v for open/close positions in the low-high interval: p_o=sigmoid(u), p_c=sigmoid(v), low=c−p_c R, high=low+R, open=low+p_o R, close=c. In log-price coordinates this preserves price ordering after exponentiation. Persistently anchored equivalents can be constructed without changing the close-return anchor. Do not enforce these inequalities on beta-adjusted coordinates unless that transform is proved order-preserving; construct physical candles first and then apply the existing evaluation transform.

**HYPOTHESIS — Caveats/value:** zero ranges and boundary touches are real tick-data outcomes. A continuous log/logit density needs proper interval/censored likelihood or boundary mass, not epsilon-clipping presented as exact likelihood. Include the transformation Jacobian for a continuous-density comparison. Nonlinear inverse transforms do not map latent means to arithmetic OHLC means; retain/directly evaluate the analytic close-return mean, and do not call a transformed median an MSE-optimal price forecast. This fixes support, not period memorization.

**HYPOTHESIS — Cost/falsifier:** the continuous four-coordinate/eight-output head has **0 parameter/projection delta**, order NHC scalar transforms, reserve **<0.02 TFLOP**, **+1–4 GB**, **+2–8 ms** for transform/Jacobian work. This budget does **not** include an unspecified multivariate censored-likelihood solver; adoption is conditional on choosing a tractable tick-aware likelihood. Cheapest falsifier is offline: measure invalid current candles and resulting change in a frozen execution/risk rule. If invalidity has no economic impact, deprioritize. A ≤5k run must improve h64 amplitude or actual execution value, not merely reduce invalid candles. Full implementation is not proposed until boundary likelihood is specified and costed.

### 7. Quantile/CRPS or mixture distributions

**HYPOTHESIS — Quantiles:** retain the Gaussian head for a comparable NLL and add three quantiles per coordinate at 0.1587, 0.5 and 0.8413 with pinball loss. Noncrossing parameterization is needed. This identifies asymmetric intervals and a median, not a conditional mean; three quantiles do not define a unique likelihood or full joint path distribution. q=2304: **+1,179,648 weights, +0.6795 TFLOP**, raw output **0.442–0.885 GB**, traffic **+4–8 GB**, latency **+9–18 ms**. Cheapest falsifier: first evaluate Gaussian-implied quantile errors; then one matched ≤5k auxiliary run. Reject if interval scores improve without h64 amplitude or h8 net gains. This is more expensive than analytic Gaussian CRPS and less motivated by the supplied calibration.

**HYPOTHESIS — Mixture:** replace each two-output marginal Gaussian with a two-component marginal Gaussian mixture (two means, two scales, one mixture logit), q=3×768=2304 extra outputs: **+1.180M weights, +0.6795 TFLOP** plus **<0.01 TFLOP** scalar density work, **+4–9 GB/step**, **+10–20 ms**. Score the analytic mixture mean, not its highest-weight component; exact scalar NLL remains available. It can express skew/multimodality, but independent mixtures still do not express channel/path dependence and can memorize additional regimes. Cheapest falsifier: train-period-fitted residual mixture versus Gaussian on held-out NLL first; then a ≤5k dynamic-mixture run only if the residual shape transfers. Reject trading applicability if shape improves but long-mean amplitude or net h8 utility does not.

## Bottom line

**HYPOTHESIS — Allocate compute to a mechanism that acts on the measured error.** The leading candidates are economically aligned short-horizon learning and an explicit restriction of long-horizon mean freedom. NextLat is a plausible but unproven regularizer; a small one-step auxiliary test is sufficient to challenge that judgment. Correlations, valid candles, quantiles and mixtures are useful modeling capabilities, but none should be sold as a fix for period-overfit means merely because it improves density or sample appearance. Preserve the improving h1/h8 band, and require costed, out-of-period trading evidence before paying for a richer target.
